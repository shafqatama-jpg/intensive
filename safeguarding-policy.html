Safeguarding Policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026

Kings Driving Centre is committed to keeping every learner safe. This policy explains how we protect learners under 18 and adults at risk, and what to do if you are worried about someone's safety or about the behaviour of anyone working with us.

Who this policy covers

It applies to every instructor and member of staff at Kings Driving Centre, and to every lesson, mock test and test-day booking, in English, German, Hindi, Urdu or Punjabi. It protects all learners, with particular care for learners under 18 and for adults who may be at risk because of a disability, illness, learning difficulty or their circumstances.

Our commitments

  • All of our instructors are DVSA Approved and DBS checked.
  • Learners are treated with respect and dignity at all times, whatever their age, sex, race, religion, disability, sexual orientation or background.
  • Instructors keep professional boundaries. Physical contact is avoided unless it is needed for safety in an emergency.
  • Instructors contact learners only about lessons, bookings and test arrangements.
  • For learners under 18, a parent or guardian is welcome to speak to us at any time about lessons and progress.
  • Lessons are adapted for nervous learners and for anyone with a specific learning difficulty, at a pace that suits them.
  • Personal information is handled as set out in our Privacy Policy.

If you are worried about something

If a learner, parent, guardian or member of the public has a concern about a learner's safety, or about the conduct of an instructor, please tell us as soon as you can. Call 020 3143 1718 or email tolworthdrivingcentre@gmail.com and ask for the safeguarding lead. You do not need proof, and you will not be treated differently for raising a concern in good faith.

If someone is in immediate danger, call 999.

What we will do

  1. Listen, take the concern seriously and write down what we are told.
  2. Share it only with the people who need to know. We cannot promise to keep a concern secret if someone may be at risk of harm.
  3. Where a child or an adult at risk may be suffering harm, pass the concern to the local council's children's or adult social care team, or to the police.
  4. Where the concern is about an instructor, look into it promptly. The instructor may be taken off lessons while this happens, and we will co-operate fully with the police, the council and the DVSA.
  5. Tell the person who raised the concern what we can about the outcome.

Other places to get help

  • Police: 999 in an emergency, 101 at other times.
  • NSPCC helpline for adults worried about a child: 0808 800 5000.
  • Childline for children and young people: 0800 1111.
  • Concerns about the conduct of a DVSA Approved Driving Instructor can also be reported to the DVSA directly.

Complaints and review

For concerns that are not about safety, please use our Complaints Procedure. We review this policy at least once a year and whenever the law or official guidance changes.
